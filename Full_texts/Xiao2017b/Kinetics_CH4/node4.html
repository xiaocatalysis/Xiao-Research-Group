Introduction

Energy security and environmental concerns are drivers for utilization of biomass to produce bio-fuels. The vast quantity of biomass available in the United States has the potential to replace significant amounts of fuels that are currently derived from petroleum sources. Bio-oil, generally derived from fast pyrolysis of biomass, is one such candidate fuel. [#Tuck2012##1###] Its production process, however, remains under investigation, owing primarily to its high oxygen content, which may cause instability, poor combustion performance and low heating value. [#Nigam2011##1###,#Elliott2009##1###,#Mortensen2011##1###] Too high levels of oxygen make pyrolysis oils distinctly different from petroleum fuels. Oxygen removal from pyrolysis bio-oils, thus, is typically followed as an upgrading process to utilize these bio-oils as transportation fuels.[#Xiu2012##1###,#Elliott2015##1###]

Since the pyrolysis bio-oil is always a mixture of more than 400 chemical species, and different biomass sources result in different bio-oil compositions, model compounds such as phenol, guaiacol (2-methoxyphenol) and benzylphenyl ether[#Guvenatam2014##1###], are typically selected to represent bio-oils for more fundamental studies[#Bridgwater2012##1###] Among these model compounds, guaiacol is attractive because it contains two common oxygenated groups in bio-oils: hydroxyl and methoxyl. Catalytic hydrodeoxygenation (HDO), as a common bio-oil upgrading approach, refers to oxygen removal by the use of H#tex2html_wrap_inline352# molecules over heterogeneous catalysts. Using Pt, Pd, Co, etc. as primary metals, [#Wildschut2009##1###] Mo, S, etc. as promoters[#Wangt2011##1###], and activated carbon (AC), Al#tex2html_wrap_inline354#O#tex2html_wrap_inline356#, ZrO#tex2html_wrap_inline358#, SiO#tex2html_wrap_inline360#, etc. as catalytst supports,[#Gutierrez2009##1###,#Saidi2014##1###,#Wu2013##1###] various experimental and theoretical studies have been reported for guaiacol catalytic HDO.[#Zhao2011##1###,#Bui2011##1###,#Zhang2013##1###,#Mochizuki2014##1###,#Olcese2012##1###,#Badawi2011##1###,#Lee2015##1###]

Although H#tex2html_wrap_inline362# is an ideal reductant for oxygen removal from pyrolysis oils, its use for HDO, however, leads to possible economic concerns owing to high cost for H#tex2html_wrap_inline364# production and transportation. For this reason, we have recently reported a novel bio-oil upgrading approach[#Xiao2015##1###], using CH#tex2html_wrap_inline366# instead of H#tex2html_wrap_inline368# as reductant and Pt-Bi/AC as catalyst, to deoxygenate guaiacol efficiently. It was found that using the mono-metallic Pt/AC catalyst, CH#tex2html_wrap_inline370# exhibited as good deoxygenation performance as H#tex2html_wrap_inline372# in terms of guaiacol conversion and product distribution, but the catalyst deactivated rapidly. With addition of bismuth as promoter, the lifetime of the bi-metallic Pt-Bi/AC was extended significantly without loss of activity, as compared to the Pt/AC catalyst. In our other prior works[#Gao2014##1###,#Gao2015##1###], using Pt/AC and H#tex2html_wrap_inline374#, reaction pathways and detailed kinetics of guaiacol deoxygenation process were reported.

On the basis of these prior studies[#Xiao2015##1###,#Gao2014##1###,#Gao2015##1###], in the present work, both experimental and modeling investigations were conducted, to reveal the reaction pathways and kinetics of guaiacol deoxygenation using methane as the reductant, Kinetics measurements in both differential and integral reactors were carried out, and rate constants and activation energy values were determined for the individual reaction steps. These results are discussed and compared with our prior studies as well as other related works.
