Absence of Mass Transfer Limitations

Before conducting kinetics measurements, mass transfer limitations were tested in a differential reactor, using the well-known procedure. [#Perego1999##1###]. The experimental results are shown in Figure 3.

Figure 3. Mass transfer limitation tests for a) internal diffusion effect and b) external diffusion effect

Figure 3a was obtained by varying packed catalyst particle diameter (#tex2html_wrap_inline440#) in the range 100 micron ;SPMlt; #tex2html_wrap_inline442# ;SPMlt; 1000 micron, while keeping other operating conditions unchanged. Figure 3b was obtained by varying feed flow rates, while maintaining contact time (W/F) and other operating conditions constant. Figure 3a shows that when catalyst particle diameter (#tex2html_wrap_inline444#) is larger than 250 micron, corresponding to 60 mesh, internal diffusion effects are discerned, while when (#tex2html_wrap_inline446#) ;SPMlt; 150 micron, corresponding to 100 mesh, no significant internal effects are found. As shown in Figure 3b, when feed flow rate is smaller than 1.2 mL/min, there is an obvious external mass transfer effect, while no such effect is observed when feed flow rate is larger than 1.5 mL/min. Thus, (#tex2html_wrap_inline448#) ;SPMlt; 150 micron and feed flow rate larger than 1.5 mL/min were used in the kinetics measurement experiments. Further, the criteria described by Weisz and Prater [#Weisz1954##1###] and Mears [#Mears1971##1###] were both satisfied to verify the absence of mass and heat transfer effects in all experiments.
