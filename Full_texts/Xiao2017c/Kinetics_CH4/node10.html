Kinetic Model Development

In the reaction pathways shown in Figure 1, each step (R1-R4) contains more than one elementary reaction. For example, step R1, when converting glycerol to acrolein, involves two sequential dehydration reactions [#!Gu2012!#,#!Zhang2015!#,#!Galadima2016b!#], while more complex reactions (e.g. hydrogenation, dehydrogenation, deoxygenation, etc.) occur when other oxygenates are produced [#!Park2015!#,#!Tamiyakul2015!#,#!Wang2016!#]. Step R2 includes deoxygenation, dehydrogenation and C-C bond cleavage reactions, while step R4 includes oligomerization, cyclization and dehydrogenation. Step 3 refers to pathways where C-C gases do not exist. Thus the overall GTH conversion comprises a complex network, which can be reduced to a few equivalent lumped reactions: steps R1-R4. As lumped pseudo-components, oxygenates represent a mixture of acetaldehyde, propanal, acrolein and hydroxyacetone. Gases represent a mixture of methane, ethylene and propylene, while aromatics refer to C-C aromatic hydrocarbons.
